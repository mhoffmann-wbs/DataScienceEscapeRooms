# World, scenes and UI

Part of the architecture notes; the rules that apply everywhere are in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md).

## World and scenes
- Art: `scripts/generate-art.mjs` draws everything. It writes `content/tilesets/tiles.png` (8 columns of 32 px tiles; maps refer to tiles by index, so append new tiles, never reorder), `content/tilesets/tiles.json` (the tile count and which tiles are **solid**: a tile is marked with `solid(...)` where it is drawn) and `content/sprites/avatar-parts.png`, and it rewrites the size of the embedded tileset in every map. Maps carry no tile properties: run the script after adding tiles. Tiles 0-207 are the original set; from 208 on each room has a block of 32 tiles for its own look (`ROOM_BLOCKS`, `blockOf(roomId)`), drawn in its own marked section. `onWall(index, src, wall)` re-hangs an existing wall-mounted tile on a different wall face, and `drawPiece` draws furniture several tiles big. Kenney's CC0 packs were considered and dropped: they have no lab, hotel or station props and no 4-direction walking characters.
- Phaser scenes: `Boot` (loads every map and the sprites) → `World`, one generic scene that shows whichever map the registry's `mapKey` names (`lobby` or a roomId). React overlays sit on top of the canvas and talk to Phaser only through the typed event bus in `src/game/events.ts`. They never reach into scene internals. To add a message, add it to the `GameEvents` map.
- The app moves between places: join screen → lobby → room (`src/ui/App.tsx`). `App` owns the transport and the `LobbySession`, which stays connected for as long as the player is in the world, **including while in a room**: presence then marks them `away` (the roomId), so they vanish from the lobby map and from "nearby" but still count for the scoreboard, instructors and rejoining. `World` (`src/ui/World.tsx`) owns one place: a fresh Phaser game, plus the `RoomSession` for rooms; it tears both down on switch. Author mode stays offline (no lobby session). Solo practice (`SavedRun.solo`, "Practice alone" at a door) plays the room on an in-process `MemoryHub` too, but keeps the lobby connection; it hides chat and voice and publishes no run summary. The current run (`dser.run`, `src/player/run.ts`) is saved on every state change (with the room password, if any). Joining again (e.g. after a reload) always starts in the lobby, with a banner (`RejoinReminder`) naming the saved run's room and door, and, from the lobby's live run summaries, how many teammates are still in the room (or that nobody is); the player rejoins it at that door. Leaving a room before escaping keeps it saved: the room's door then offers Rejoin (merging with the team's current state) or Discard. The team's creator (`SavedRun.creator`, also set for solo practice) can instead **end the run** from the leave dialog: `RunSync.end()` sets `endedAt`, the creator's saved run is cleared, and teammates in the room see "The run has ended" and go back to the lobby with theirs cleared too. The creator also announces the end in the lobby (`Runs.end`), so a teammate who was away learns of it when they come back: the reload banner says so, and the door dialog drops their saved run instead of offering Rejoin. Only if nobody who heard it is online by then can they still continue the run.
- Doors, puzzle objects (terminals, safes, keypads), clues and spawn points are **Tiled object-layer objects** with custom properties (`doorTo`, `puzzleId`, `clueId`). The engine reads these properties. It never uses hard-coded coordinates. The scene treats doors, puzzles, clues and the handbook alike as interactables: it emits `interact:changed`/`interact:use` on the bus, and React decides what happens. React sends `puzzles:solved` back so the scene can draw ✓ markers; it re-sends on `scene:ready`.
- Map conventions, read by `src/game/tiled.ts`:
  - Tile layers are named `floor` and `walls`, plus two optional ones: `decor` between them (rugs, stripes, puddles, footprints, glow: drawn over the floor and under the furniture) and `above`, drawn over the avatars but under their name labels (hanging lamps, canopies, luggage racks). A tile blocks movement when it is on `walls` and solid in `tiles.json`; `decor` and `above` never block, and a test keeps solid tiles off them.
  - The object layer is named `objects`. Objects are identified by their class (`type`/`class` in the JSON): `spawn` (a point, which gets a small random offset, or a rectangle players arrive somewhere inside; players coming back from a room arrive one tile in front of that room's lobby door instead, see `inFrontOf`), `door` (a rectangle with a `doorTo`: in the lobby a room type from `content/lobby/doors.json`, in a room `lobby` for its exit; optionally `facing`: `down` by default for doors in the top wall, `right` for a door in the left wall, `left` for one in the right wall. The facing sets where returning players arrive and where the lobby sign goes: above a top door, in the wall-top row; beside a side door, turned on its side in the outer of the lobby's two wall columns (the lobby's side walls are its top wall turned a quarter: wall top outside, tiles 112/113, and wall front inside, tiles 114/115, with the door in the inner column). Doors and room exits share a colour per room type. Top-wall doors (tiles 5 Python, 193 machine learning, 194 neural networks; tiles 6 and 43 are the older SQL and time series ones, no longer placed) lead to rooms whose exit is also in their top wall. Side doors are two tiles tall and come in pairs, one for a left wall and one mirrored for a right wall: 54/55 statistics, 181/182 SQL, 191/192 time series (105/106 neural networks and the old crimson machine learning pair are no longer placed). The SQL and statistics doors in the lobby's left wall lead to rooms whose exit is in their right wall, the time series door in its right wall to rooms whose exit is in their left wall; a side exit sits on two rows near the middle of the wall that have free floor in front) `puzzle` (a rectangle with a `puzzleId`, covering the object's tile and the tile in front of it, where the player stands), `clue` (shaped the same, with a `clueId`), `reference` (shaped the same, no properties: where the room's handbook stands, exactly one per room map; draw it with tile 77, the lectern, or a themed stand from the room's block, somewhere players pass early, e.g. near the spawn) and `area` (a named rectangle: a private area, see [Proximity](networking.md#proximity)).
  - The lobby map is `content/lobby/map.json`, and room maps are `content/rooms/<roomId>/map.json`, discovered by `src/game/maps.ts`. The lobby (28 x 22) has three room-type doors in its top wall (from left to right Python, machine learning, neural networks), two in its left wall (SQL at the top, statistics below) and one in its right wall (time series), each with stone in front and a wall lamp on both sides (tile 135, turned for the side walls: 140 left, 141 right). Players spawn at a decorative glass entrance in the bottom wall (tiles 136-137, with the doormat 138-139 on the walls layer, not collidable). A red runner joins the doors, the entrance and the openings into the bar (bottom left) and the café (bottom right), which are walled off with inner walls (142 running down the room, 143 its end above an opening). The runner picks each tile from its neighbours: 144 + a mask of the sides where it ends (1 top, 2 right, 4 bottom, 8 left), or 160-163 for a borderless tile with an inside corner (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right), so strips of any shape join up. The lounge rug is nine tiles (164-172: corners, edges and centre, row by row). Its two lounges, the bar and the café are the lobby's private areas. The lounge and bar furniture is tiles 116-134; the seats (sofas 116-119, armchairs 120-121, bar stools 132) are not collidable, so players can walk onto them and sit, the rest is. Tests run over every map: the spawn is walkable and not on a door, doors are reachable, every door, puzzle, clue and handbook can be walked to from the spawn, the lobby has a door to every room, and every room has an exit.
- Maps and images are bundled through Vite imports (`import url from '…png'`, `import map from '…json'`) rather than `public/`. This keeps them hashed and base-path-safe.
- Keyboard input: the scenes register keys with `enableCapture = false` and ignore movement while an HTML field has focus (`isTypingInUi`). Keep it this way, or chat and form inputs break. Keys: E uses doors and objects (a lobby door's dialog opens on its own when the player steps on it, and closes when they step off; E reopens it); Enter opens chat, Enter again sends and returns to walking, Esc leaves the chat.
- The game and React share one `Session`. The scene reads it from the Phaser registry (`src/game/registry.ts`); components subscribe with `useEmitterValue` (`src/ui/useStore.ts`). Presence's `roster` event fires only on join/leave, so React doesn't re-render at position-update rate.
- Avatars are customizable: a `Look` (`src/game/avatars.ts`) picks skin tone, hair style and colour, top style and colour, trouser colour and accessory (none, glasses, cap, headphones, lab goggles), 5 options each. `avatar-parts.png` has one body row per hair style × top style, then one overlay row per accessory, drawn over the body (the cap takes the top colour); its columns are 4 directions × 3 frames (idle, step, other step; the walk cycle is `WALK_FRAMES`). Recolourable parts are drawn in marker colours that encode a shade (`[v,0,0]` hair, `[0,v,0]` top, `[0,0,v]` trousers, `[v,0,v]` skin, with v = shade × 100), and `renderLook` swaps them for the look's palette colours. Phaser makes one texture per look on first use (`Avatar.ts`), and the React `AvatarPreview` the same strip as a data URL. To add an option, append to the lists in both `avatars.ts` and the generator (the row order must match), and keep every fixed colour's channels above zero. Profiles from before looks existed (`avatar: 0–11`) are migrated with `legacyLook`.
- The camera may scroll past the map edges by the height of the React top bar and status bar (`UI_INSET_*` in `WorldScene`), so the overlays never hide parts of the map.
- Chat messages also appear as speech bubbles over the sender, if they are on the same map: `ChatBubbles` forwards new ones as `chat:bubble` on the bus, and `Avatar.say` shows them for a few seconds.
- The chat panel can be collapsed to a bar with an unread count; Enter reopens it. Clicking the map blurs any focused field, so walking works again; text fields also leave on Esc (chat, notes) and on Enter after sending.
- `Avatar` (`src/game/entities/`) is the sprite plus name label. The local player adds an arcade body sized to the feet; remote players will reuse the class without physics.
- Lobby doors are **room types**, not rooms: `content/lobby/doors.json` lists each door's id (its `doorTo`), icon, title and planned scenarios (`upcoming`, shown greyed out as "Coming soon"). `WorldScene` draws the icon and title as a sign above each lobby door. Each room's manifest names its door in `category` and its `difficulty` (five tiers, easiest first: `beginner`/`elementary`/`intermediate`/`advanced`/`expert`); `scenariosAt(doorId)` (`src/rooms/doors.ts`) lists a door's scenarios, playable first, each group easiest first, with ties in the order of the door's optional `order` list of room ids. Tests check that the lobby has exactly one door per type and every room's type has a door.
- Team flow: when a player steps on a lobby door, a dialog opens automatically (and closes when they walk away). It lets them pick a scenario (clicking one selects it; its ⓘ button shows its `about`: story, what it practises, tools) and create a team for it (public, or private with a password; optionally tagged with a group such as a class name) or join a team waiting at that door for any of its scenarios (🔒 teams ask for the password). Entering then switches the player to that team's room instance.
- Enter does not open the chat while a modal (`aria-modal`) is open or a code editor has focus.
